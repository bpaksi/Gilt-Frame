## The woman
**Name, dates:** Lena Rader Meijer (b. 14 May 1919, farm near Lakeview MI; d. 15 Jan 2022, age 102) [1][2]
**Supporting figure (same place):** Edgar Degas, whose bronzes are in the permanent collection [3]
**Order title (fiction):** "The Gardener."
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- The Lena Meijer Tropical Conservatory opened in 1995; it is five stories and 15,000 sq ft [1].
- The grounds include a three-quarter-scale replica of her childhood farm home with a restored windmill; the rose bushes came from her own garden [1].
- She was hired as a cashier at the original Meijer store in Greenville and married Fred Meijer in 1946 [3].
- Memorial gifts at her death were directed to her Children's Garden [1].
**Link type:** Order attribution (the Order claims her garden as one of its places; history records the garden, not the Order).

## Inbound clue
"A farm girl grew up to plant a garden so large a whole city walks in it. In the middle she put back the house she was born in, windmill and all, and planted her own roses beside it. Go and find her home."

## Narrative
- **Base:** She came from a farm and never forgot it. When she had the means to build anything, she built a garden and put her childhood home in the middle of it, roses from her own yard at the door. People walk past it on their way to the sculptures. You stopped at the house.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** Most people come here for the bronzes and the glass house. You found the small farmhouse and the windmill turning beside it. That is what the Order looks for. Someone who finds the quiet thing.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** It takes nerve to show the world where you came from, plainly, a farmhouse among famous sculptures. She did. To see clearly is to let yourself be seen as you are.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** Gardens are the most fragile art there is. Frost, drought, one bad season. She built one anyway, the largest she could. Everything we love fully, we agree to lose. She planted it anyway.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** She lived to be a hundred and two, and this garden grew up around her the whole time. She was watched over, and loved, long before she knew how much. So have you been, Sparrow.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** She is gone now. Her roses still bloom by the door of the house she came from. Nothing that matters lasts. The roses come back each year anyway. That is the point.
