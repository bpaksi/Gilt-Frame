## The woman
**Name, dates:** Lucinda Hinsdale Stone (30 Sep 1814, Hinesburg VT - 14 Mar 1900) [1]
**Order title (fiction):** "The Teacher."
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- Organized the Ladies' Library Association [1].
- The building, finished 1879, is called "the first building erected as a woman's club in the country"; it is a National Historic Landmark [1].
- She chose the paintings that still hang there [1].
- Taught women and men together on the same curriculum at Kalamazoo College; administrators objected, including to her teaching Byron. The Stones resigned in late 1863. She then ran a school from her home until it burned in 1866 [1].
- Took young women to Europe to study art and culture [1].
- Spoke at the 1893 World's Fair in Chicago for women's clubs [1]. (A true link to the Fair; usable in copy only if it does not name another station.)
- The Association commissioned a stained-glass memorial window honoring Ruth Webster, who gave the lot and served 24 years as treasurer and 15 as librarian [2].
**Link type:** True link.

## Inbound clue
"Women in this town once built a house for books with their own money, when no one would build one for them. The woman who filled its walls with paintings lost her own house to fire and kept teaching. Find the house they built."

## Narrative
- **Base:** She was told what women should not learn, and she taught it anyway. When they pushed her out, she taught at home. When home burned, she built this. The paintings on these walls are the ones she chose for the women who would come after her. You are one of them.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** Most people in this town have driven past this building and never wondered who built it. You came inside. She chose every painting here so someone like you would stand still in front of it.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** They objected to what she taught, and to whom. She did not argue the point. She kept teaching and let them do what they would. It cost her a post. It did not cost her the work.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** She lost her school. Then she lost her house to fire. Each time she built again, knowing what building costs. Everything you allow yourself to truly see, you give the power to hurt you. She chose to see anyway.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** She took young women across an ocean to stand in front of paintings, because she believed they were worth the trouble. Someone once believed that about you, Sparrow. You were noticed long before you were asked.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** The women who raised this hall are gone. The window for the one who gave the ground still holds the light. The Order keeps the names that the world lets slip.
