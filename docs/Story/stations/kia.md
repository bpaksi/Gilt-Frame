## The woman
**Name, dates:** Anna Louise Raymond [1]; dates [UNVERIFIED, ask KIA library].
**Supporting women (same place):** Kathryn Wood, the city's most prominent artist, proposed the KIA in 1923 [1]; Blanche Hull, first board president [2]; Alice Walton, founder of Art Bridges, whose Access for All program funds KIA's free Thursdays [3].
**Order title (fiction):** "The Benefactor."
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- The KIA began in one rented YWCA room with small budgets and volunteer staff [1].
- Raymond gave much of her collection and a $20,000 endowment in 1925 "that became critical to the KIA's survival" [1].
- The KIA owns Mary Cassatt prints (shown in the 2018 "Passion on Paper" exhibition) [4].
- Free Thursdays are funded by Art Bridges Foundation's Access for All, "founded by philanthropist Alice Walton" [3].

## Inbound clue
"In a rented room, with borrowed chairs and no money, a few women started a museum. It nearly failed. One of them gave what she had and it did not. You know the doors. You may not know who held them open."

## Narrative
- **Base:** It began as one room and a handful of women who would not let it go. One of them gave nearly everything she had collected so the doors would stay open. They have stayed open. People walk through them every day and never ask who to thank.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** You walk these halls more than most. You know where the light falls in each room. The women who opened this place hoped someone would know it like that.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** A museum in a rented room is a foolish thing to start. They started it anyway, with volunteers and very little money. To want beauty for strangers takes nerve. So does standing in front of strangers and helping them see it.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** It nearly closed. One gift saved it. Everything built for love can fail. They built it anyway, and someone stepped forward when it mattered.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** You have stood in these galleries and taught others to look. Someone has been watching you do it, Sparrow. The chain you have been following runs through this building. It always ran through you.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** The women who founded this place are gone. Exhibitions come and go. The walls change every season. What stays is the practice of seeing, carried by people. You are one of them.
- **Final Frame setting line:** A gallery after the visitors have left, a Thursday evening, the doors still open. (Research file recommends this place for the reveal; that is a slot decision, not this card's.)
