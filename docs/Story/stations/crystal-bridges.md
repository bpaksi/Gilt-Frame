## The woman
**Name, dates:** Alice Walton (b. 1949, living) [1]
**Supporting woman (same place):** Mary Cassatt, through *The Reader* (1877) [1][2]
**Order title (fiction):** "The Builder." (The old design called the place "the vault"; that name can stay in station copy.)
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- Walton built Crystal Bridges, which opened in 2011 [1].
- A near-fatal car accident in 1983 was followed by years of surgeries; art books and watercolors helped her recover, and that experience is part of why the museum exists [1].
- *The Reader* by Mary Cassatt, 1877, oil on canvas, 32 x 25 1/2 in. It shows a young woman absorbed in a book with a bent cover, wearing a white garment and a red-accented hat. She does not look at the viewer. It is signed "M S Cassatt / 1877" [1].
- In 1877 Edgar Degas invited Cassatt to exhibit with the Impressionists [1].
- Crystal Spring on the grounds flows at a constant 52 F [1].
**Link type:** True link (Walton collects Cassatt; the museum is real and documented).

## Inbound clue
"Far to the south, a woman who was nearly lost to an accident built a house for art in the woods, where no one expected one. Among her treasures a young woman reads and does not look up. Go and find her."

## Narrative
- **Base:** She was broken once, badly, and art brought her back. So she built a place in the trees where anyone could walk in and be brought back too. Somewhere inside, a painted woman reads a book she has read many times. She does not need you to look. You will anyway.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** The woman in the painting is not looking at you. She is somewhere else entirely, inside her book. You noticed the bent cover. Most people never get past the hat.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** Most people who are hurt that badly close up. She opened a door instead, in a place no one thought could hold it. To see clearly after you have been hurt takes more than nerve. It takes choosing to.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** She nearly lost everything once. She built anyway, and filled it with things that can be lost. That is the bargain the Order has always made. Love what can be taken. Keep it open to everyone.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** The reader does not know she is being watched. She has been watched for well over a century, kindly, by strangers who stop in front of her. You have been seen like that, Sparrow, while you were busy with something you loved.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** The painting comes and goes from the walls. The spring outside never changes. Everything we keep, we keep for a while. That is why we bring you to see it now.
- **Final Frame setting line:** The bridges over the water at dusk, glass glowing in the woods.
