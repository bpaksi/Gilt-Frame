**Status:** Candidate (planned day trip; tour booking required)

## The woman
**Name, dates:** Ellen Warren Scripps Booth (10 Jul 1863 - 24 Jan 1948) [1]
**Order title (fiction):** "The Quiet Hand."
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- Co-founded Cranbrook and advised on its art purchases [1].
- "By nature shy and retiring"; her influence was "less publicized" [1].
- After her death her account books revealed "many hundreds of previously undisclosed gifts" [1].
- The family moved to Cranbrook on 1 June 1908, their 21st wedding anniversary [1].
**Link type:** Order attribution (the Order claims her quiet giving as its own practice; history records the giving, not the Order).

## Inbound clue
"A shy woman gave away more than anyone knew. They only found out when someone opened her account books. Go to the house she helped build and ask what she kept quiet."

## Narrative
- **Base:** She did not want to be thanked. She gave, wrote it down in a book no one read, and went on. Hundreds of gifts. No one knew until she was gone. The Order understands that kind of love. It is the kind we practice.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** Most visitors come for the house. You looked for the woman who stayed out of the photographs. That is what attention is, Sparrow. Seeing who is missing from the picture.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** It is easier to give where people can see. She gave where no one could, and never corrected anyone who thought less of her for it. That takes a courage most people never need.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** To give in secret is to risk never being known for it. She took that risk her whole life. Everything given fully can be lost without a trace. She gave anyway.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** Her gifts were seen only after, in her own careful hand. Every one of them had been seen by her, and remembered. You have been seen that way too, quietly, for years, and someone has been keeping the account.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** She was gone before anyone opened the books. The thanks came too late for her to hear. Nothing lasts long enough. That is not the tragedy. That is the reason to say it now.
