**Status:** Candidate (needs recon: gate hours, plot location)

**Address & GPS:** North side of W. Main St between Ingleside Terrace and Hilbert St, Kalamazoo [1]. GPS not scouted.

## The woman
**Name, dates:** Lucinda Hinsdale Stone (1814-1900) [1][3], buried here with James A. B. Stone [1].
**Alternate:** Caroline Bartlett Crane, Kalamazoo reformer, also buried here [1]; her dates are [UNVERIFIED].
**Order title (fiction):** "The Teacher" (same as the Ladies' Library card) or, if Crane, a new title TBD.
**Real fact(s) used in play:**
- Stone's gravesite "had become unkempt." In 2006 members of People's Church restored and rededicated it [1].
- The cemetery has an 1878 Gothic receiving vault [1].
**Link type:** True link.

## Inbound clue
"There is a hill on the west side of town where a teacher's grave was let go to weeds, and then, a century on, strangers came and set it right. Go and see what they kept."

## Narrative
- **Base:** For a long time no one came here. The grass grew over her name. Then people who never met her decided she should not be forgotten, and they came with tools. That is all the Order has ever been. People who choose to keep watch.
- **Beat 1 Attention:** Most people come here for one name. You read the others on the way. Every stone is a person someone once looked at closely. You understand that already.
- **Beat 2 Courage:** It is not easy to walk among the dead and keep your eyes open. Most people hurry. You did not. To see clearly asks something of you, and here it asks the most.
- **Beat 3 Risk:** She risked everything she had for what she believed, and lost it more than once. Even her grave was nearly lost. To be fully seen is also to be fully forgettable. She was neither, in the end.
- **Beat 4 Being seen:** Strangers saw her a hundred years after she was gone and decided she mattered. You are seen like that, Sparrow, and not by strangers. Someone has been tending you for a long time.
- **Beat 5 Loss:** Every soul the Order has loved was taken from it. This is where that becomes plain. The light that falls on this hill today will not fall the same way tomorrow. That is not the tragedy. That is why we brought you here.
